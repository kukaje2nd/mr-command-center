Parameter Reference

What the controls mean beyond the simplified model

Directional teaching notes for the parameters and setup variables that sit around the Lab. Use this as a quick reference—not a source of protocol values.

sequence dependent
Related tools
Open supporting material only when the workspace raises the question.
How to use this deskOpen one category at a time. Each concept separates the usual directional effect from the sequence/scanner details that the simplified Lab cannot decide.
GGeometry & coverage5 concepts · coverage · matrix · slice+
Frequency matrixMore readout samples shrink the frequency-direction pixel at the same FOV. Real effects on readout duration, gradient demand, bandwidth per pixel, and minimum TE depend on sequence implementation.
Phase matrixMore phase encodes shrink the phase-direction pixel and usually increase acquisition burden when other acceleration factors are unchanged.
Phase FOVControls displayed/sampled phase-direction coverage. Reducing it can improve phase-direction pixel size for a fixed matrix but raises aliasing risk if anatomy extends beyond the covered field.
Slice thickness / gapThinner slices improve through-plane detail but reduce voxel signal. Gap and slice-interleaving behavior are sequence dependent and are not modeled here.
Phase oversampling / no-wrapCan reduce phase aliasing by sampling more outside the nominal FOV, usually with some acquisition or reconstruction cost depending on implementation.
SSignal & noise4 concepts · averaging · BW · acceleration · coil+
NEX / NSA / averagesRepeated measurements improve averaging efficiency approximately with the square root of averages while acquisition burden generally rises roughly linearly.
Receiver bandwidthHigher bandwidth generally reduces chemical-shift displacement and susceptibility-related frequency-direction distortion, while lowering SNR efficiency.
Parallel accelerationReduces phase-encoding burden by using coil sensitivity information. Real SNR loss depends on acceleration, coil geometry, g-factor, reference lines, and reconstruction—not just the selected R value.
Coil / positioningCoil selection, enabled elements, centering, and anatomy-to-coil distance can dominate SNR and are not represented by a parameter slider.
TTime & efficiency4 concepts · ETL · PF · coverage · gating+
Echo train length / turbo factorLonger trains can reduce acquisition time in FSE/TSE-family imaging, but may increase train-related blurring and alter effective contrast.
Partial FourierAcquires less than full k-space and reconstructs the missing portion. It can reduce acquisition burden, but noise and artifact behavior depend on the reconstruction and phase consistency.
Number of slices / slab coverageCoverage affects total sequence burden differently across 2D, 3D, interleaved, segmented, and accelerated acquisitions, so it is not included in the universal time proxy.
Gating / triggeringCan improve consistency with periodic physiology, but changes effective timing and scan duration according to the physiologic cycle and acceptance window.
CContrast timing4 concepts · TR · TE · TI · flip angle+
TRRepetition time strongly influences longitudinal recovery, contrast, and often scan time, but its meaning differs across spin-echo, GRE, inversion, EPI, 3D, and steady-state families.
TEEcho time changes transverse weighting and susceptibility / dephasing exposure. Minimum and effective TE behavior are highly sequence dependent.
TIInversion time selects where longitudinal magnetization sits during inversion-recovery imaging and is commonly chosen around a suppression or contrast goal.
Flip angleChanges excitation / refocusing behavior, signal, contrast, and RF energy. There is no single “more is better” direction across sequence families.
AArtifact & encoding5 concepts · phase · echo spacing · shim · suppression+
Phase-encode directionControls the direction of many motion/flow ghosts and phase aliasing. Swapping it can move an artifact away from critical anatomy but may create a different conflict.
Echo spacingEspecially important in EPI and long echo trains; longer spacing generally increases off-resonance distortion and train-related effects.
Shimming / center frequencyField homogeneity and frequency setup influence fat suppression, EPI distortion, susceptibility behavior, and spectral selectivity. They are setup variables rather than universal image-quality sliders.
Fat suppression methodFrequency-selective fat sat, STIR, Dixon-family methods, and other techniques have different robustness, contrast, timing, and field-homogeneity tradeoffs.
Flow compensation / saturationCan reduce specific flow artifacts or unwanted signal, but may add timing, coverage, or contrast costs depending on the sequence.
