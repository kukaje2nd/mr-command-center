About MR Command Center

Interactive MRI reasoning, with the model boundaries visible.

MR Command Center is a browser-based collection of interactive teaching Labs for exploring MRI relationships: parameter tradeoffs, contrast, timing, motion, k-space, spatial encoding, aliasing, artifacts, and supporting safety/reference concepts.

Created by Edon KukajBrowser-local workspaceStatic PWAOpen source

What it is

A manipulation-first learning environment. Each active Lab is designed around a simple loop: change a meaningful variable, observe a modeled consequence, then connect that change to the underlying MRI relationship.

  • Seven active free MRI reasoning Labs
  • Parameter comparison, presets, and local continuity
  • Contextual MRI reference and troubleshooting support
  • Public Premium concept previews with purchases disabled until secure server-side entitlement infrastructure exists

What it is not

MRCC is not a scanner console, protocol generator, diagnostic imaging system, device-clearance engine, or substitute for institutional MR safety review.

  • No patient-specific clearance
  • No diagnostic adequacy claims
  • No scanner-specific protocol prescriptions
  • No replacement for manufacturer labeling, scanner IFU/limits, authoritative MR safety guidance, or local policy

Model philosophy

The Labs deliberately simplify. Outputs are educational relationships or synthetic visualizations, not validated scanner predictions. When a real implementation depends on sequence family, coil geometry, reconstruction, hardware limits, field strength, vendor behavior, or patient/device conditions, MRCC says so explicitly.

Architecture

The current free application is intentionally dependency-light: static HTML/CSS/JavaScript, a PWA service worker, and browser-local workspace state. There is no patient-data backend. Active Lab state can be exported manually as a JSON workspace backup.

Educational boundary: Use MRCC to reason about concepts, not to authorize a scan or validate a clinical protocol. Real-world decisions remain governed by appropriate clinical supervision, manufacturer documentation, scanner limits, authoritative guidance, and local policy.
